1. Executive Summary
Venue OS is a purpose-built SaaS platform for banquet halls. It is not a generic CRM. The long-term product is intended to become the operating system for banquet-hall sales and operations, with modules added over time.
V1 focuses only on Lead Management. The first problem to solve is lead follow-up and conversation continuity: when a customer talks to one team member today and another team member tomorrow, the second person should be able to open the lead and immediately understand what was discussed previously, what the customer wants, and what the next action is.
The core interaction is intentionally simple: open lead -> read discussion history -> call or WhatsApp customer -> add a short manual discussion note -> set next follow-up. No AI-generated summaries or complex workflow automation are required in V1.
2. Product Vision
Venue OS should become the central workspace a banquet hall uses to manage its customer journey from first inquiry through booking and post-booking operations.
V1: Lead Management
Future modules: Bookings, Quotations, Calendar/Availability, Payments, Customers, Vendors/Operations, Reports, and other banquet-hall workflows
All future modules should share the same customer, event, venue, and team data model where appropriate.
The product UI should look and feel like a professional business application, not like a WhatsApp clone. WhatsApp is an external communication channel, not the primary product metaphor.

4. V1 Goals
Create one reliable record for every banquet-hall lead.
Preserve a chronological history of customer discussions.
Make the current customer context understandable in seconds.
Make follow-up scheduling quick and visible.
Give each lead clear ownership inside the team.
Reduce missed follow-ups and repeated questions to customers.
Provide a product foundation that can support future banquet-hall modules.

6. Core User Problem
A banquet-hall lead can interact with multiple team members across several days. The sales process loses context when information remains scattered across WhatsApp chats, memory, notebooks, spreadsheets, or personal phones. Venue OS centralizes that context in the lead record.
The primary question the product must answer is:
"What happened with this customer last time, and what should we do next?"

9.5 Discussion Timeline
This is the most important feature in V1.
Every discussion entry is manually added by a team member.
Each entry stores author, timestamp, and discussion text.
Entries are shown in reverse chronological order, with the latest interaction most visible.
The discussion text should be short and plain. No mandatory structured form is required.
Users can edit/delete their own recent entries according to role policy; admin can correct entries when necessary.
Example
Rahul - 25 Aug, 4:30 PM: Customer wants a 300-person reception in November. Asked for package pricing and hall photos.
Akash - 24 Aug, 6:15 PM: Shared pricing. Customer is discussing the date with family and requested a callback tomorrow.
9.6 Follow-up
Each lead can have one active next follow-up in V1.
Follow-up includes date and optional time.
Optional follow-up note can explain why the call is needed.
System visually marks Today, Upcoming, and Overdue.
Overdue follow-ups remain visible until completed, rescheduled, or cleared.

High - requires attention / strong conversion potential or urgent date.

9.9 Call and WhatsApp Actions
Call button should invoke the device/browser phone action when supported.
WhatsApp button should open a WhatsApp conversation using the stored phone number.
These are quick actions and should not replace the discussion log; the team still records the outcome manually.
9.10 Search and Filters
Search by customer name.
Search by phone number.
Search discussion text / keywords.
Filter by status, assigned member, lead source, event type, priority, and follow-up state.
9.11 Duplicate Detection
Phone number is the primary duplicate identifier in V1.
When a matching active lead exists, show a clear warning and link to the existing lead.
Do not silently merge records.
9.12 Archive
Archive converted or lost/closed leads without deleting them.
Archived leads remain searchable.
Archive should be reversible by authorized users.
9.13 Team Activity
Record who added a discussion.
Record status, assignment, and follow-up changes.
Show timestamps for all activity.
No @mentions or notification system in V1.

11. Database Design - Supabase
Supabase should be used as the backend platform, with PostgreSQL as the system of record. The relational model fits Venue OS because future modules will share customers, users, leads, bookings, quotations, events, and payments.

Important relational rules: every tenant-owned row must include organization_id; lead_discussions and lead_activity belong to a lead; owner_id and author_id point to users/profiles; future modules should reference customer/lead/event identifiers rather than duplicating data.
12. Multi-Tenant SaaS Foundation
Venue OS is intended as a SaaS product, so tenant isolation must be designed from V1.
Every organization (banquet hall business) must have its own data boundary.
Supabase Row Level Security (RLS) should enforce organization-level access.
Users must never be able to query another organization's leads, discussions, or activity through the client.
All sensitive authorization checks must be enforced server-side/database-side, not only by UI logic.
13. Authentication and Authorization
Use Supabase Auth for authentication.
Start with email/password or magic-link authentication depending on implementation preference.
Use role-based access: Owner/Admin, Manager, Sales/Team Member.
V1 does not need highly granular permission matrices.
All authenticated API/data requests must be scoped to the active organization.

18. V1 Performance Requirements
Lead list should feel instant for normal tenant sizes.
Common searches should return promptly without full-page reloads.
Adding a discussion should save and render without requiring navigation away from the lead.
Dashboard should avoid expensive unbounded queries.
Pagination or cursor-based loading should be used for large lead histories.
19. Security Requirements
Use Supabase Auth for identity.
Enforce tenant isolation with PostgreSQL RLS.
Validate and authorize all mutations server-side.
Do not expose service-role keys in the browser.
Normalize/validate phone numbers before duplicate checks.
Log sensitive state changes such as assignment/status updates.
Use least-privilege database access patterns.
20. Error and Empty States
No leads: explain how to create the first lead.
No search results: show a clear empty state and reset-search action.
Duplicate phone: show existing lead and provide a clear path to open it.
Save failure: preserve entered text and allow retry.
Permission denied: explain that the user does not have access.
Network failure: show a non-blocking error and retry option where feasible.

25. V1 Definition of Done
A tenant can create and manage banquet-hall leads.
A team member can be assigned to a lead.
A user can call or open WhatsApp from the lead.
A user can record a short discussion after each interaction.
Discussion history persists chronologically and shows author + timestamp.
A user can set and update a next follow-up.
The system clearly shows due and overdue follow-ups.
A user can change status and priority.
A user can search and filter leads.
Duplicate phone numbers are detected.
Leads can be archived and recovered by authorized users.
Admin/manager/team-member access is scoped correctly.
RLS prevents cross-tenant access.
The app is deployed and usable in production through Vercel + Supabase.
26. Example End-to-End Scenario
A Meta advertisement generates an inquiry for a wedding reception. In V1, the team creates the lead manually and records the event type, desired date, guest count, and customer requirement. A salesperson calls the customer and writes a two-line discussion summary. The salesperson schedules a follow-up for tomorrow. The next day, another team member opens the lead, reads the previous discussion, sees the follow-up, taps Call, completes the conversation, writes the new summary, and schedules the next action. No one needs to ask the previous salesperson what happened.

28. Final Product Direction
Venue OS should begin as a focused banquet-hall lead management product, but its architecture should establish the long-term foundation for a complete banquet-hall operating system. The first version should win by being easy to use, fast to update, and excellent at preserving customer conversation context.
The V1 product promise is simple:
Open the lead. Understand the customer. Know the next action.
